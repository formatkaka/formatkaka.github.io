title: 'Movie Review: Front of the Class (2008)'
description: 'A review of the inspiring film about Brad Cohen and Tourette syndrome'
pubDate: 2026-09-11
category: 'movies'
---

I came across this through a YouTube video and didn't know much about Brad Cohen or Tourette's syndrome going in. It just felt motivational enough to watch. Glad I did.

The movie follows Brad's journey from childhood all the way to becoming a teacher — and the time jumps between those phases worked fine. Nothing felt jarring or rushed. The pacing held up the whole way through, and I stayed hooked from start to finish.

What really stood out was how genuine everything felt. The cast, the situations, and the classroom moments — none of it came across as gimmicky. The curiosity and questions the kids asked Brad felt real, not scripted-cute. His straightforward approach with his students, and how quickly they accepted and loved him, was one of the most emotional parts of the film for me.

The core message — *don't let Tourette's win* — runs through the entire movie. It's that “obstacle is the way” mindset, and the film earns it. It's not always feel-good, though. There are genuinely difficult scenes that show the struggle, and that balance is what makes the motivational moments land. The film isn't pushing a glossy version of the story. It's a mix of both.

There's no single moment where the message hit home — the whole movie is a constant struggle — but the ending, where his students raise their hands in the middle of his speech, made all of it worth it. That scene pulled everything together.

If you've seen *Little Miss Sunshine*, it has a similar underdog energy. *Front of the Class* is that kind of film — real, warm, and genuinely moving without being over the top.
