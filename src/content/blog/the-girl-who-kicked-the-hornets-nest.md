### PICKING UP WHERE IT LEFT OFF

Having read the first two books, picking up the third was a no-brainer. The second book ended on a cliffhanger with too many questions left unanswered about Salander's history — there was no way I wasn't seeing this through.

The book picks up exactly where the second left off: Salander with a bullet in her head, Zalachenko hit by an axe, both taken to hospital. The first half is mostly plot development — slower, methodical — but the second half the pace picks up like crazy. The turning point for me was when Blomkvist and his team realize there's a larger conspiracy at play and start actually connecting the dots about what happened. That's when it gets really, really interesting. It also happens to coincide with Salander recovering enough to flex her computer skills from her hospital bed, which helps.

### SALANDER UNGUARDED

What makes this book different from the first two is how much we see of Salander as a person. She's always been someone who ran from her past and never talked about it. Here, at Blomkvist's request, she starts writing down her childhood experiences — and as anyone who's had a traumatic story to tell knows, once you start writing, you can't really stop. That journey of her being honest and true to herself about what really happened is one of the most compelling parts of the book. The first book made her feel like just a rebellious young person. This one binds all three together and brings everything into perspective.

### JUSTICE AND THE PEOPLE DELIVERING IT

On the justice side, Blomkvist wasn't just after revenge — he wanted to deliver real, lasting justice, and that distinction matters. Erika Berger also stood out, her ambition and honesty to the profession coming through clearly in this book. She's easy to root for.

### CLOSING THOUGHTS

As a closing chapter to the trilogy, it mostly delivers. The ending gave me the closure I was looking for. My main gripe is that I would have loved to see more of Salander — and on Blomkvist's end, I was curious to see his personal affairs develop further, along with Millennium's story and the public reaction to the report. Those threads felt like they deserved a bit more room.

Still, a good ending to a great trilogy.
